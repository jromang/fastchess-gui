A game followed in *Live* shows the engines' whole search, each move's depth and time, the opening and the tablebases'
verdict; the big screen shows the engines' lines.

## Live

- With room enough (one game, a large panel), each engine has a card: its logo grows up to the height of its name and
  clock, it says what the engine does (thinking, or the move it played), then its search figure by figure (depth and
  seldepth, score, nodes, speed, tablebase hits and hash fill when the engine sends them, the time spent on the move)
  and its whole principal variation, wrapped. Beside the mosaic, the panel keeps its short line, with the tablebase
  hits, the hash and the time added.
- *Score of the side to move* | *Score of White*, at the right of the tab: the scores of the tab from the side to
  move's point of view, as before and as fastchess writes them in the PGN, or from White's. The evaluation bar and the
  big screen stay on White's side.
- The move list gives each engine move its score, depth and time (`+0.35 d22 · 2.50s`). Hovering over a move adds when
  the engine settled on it and the reply it expected, the second move of its line: fastchess plays without pondering,
  so there are no ponder statistics. Book moves say *book*, and a line marks the end of the book.
- Above the moves: the opening, the ECO code and name of the deepest named position the game reached, engine moves
  included, with the last book move; and once the tournament's Syzygy tables know the position, their verdict, such as
  *Tablebases: White wins* with the DTZ (the half-moves to the next capture or pawn move: Syzygy tables have no
  distance to mate), or a draw. The tables are those of the tablebase adjudication, else those given to the engines;
  each position is probed once, away from the games.

## Big screen

- Under the search, the engines' lines: the side to move's as it searches, in yellow, and the other's from its last
  move, two lines each at most. *Lines*, among the controls, hides them. The opening and the tablebases' verdict are
  there too.
- A name too long for its place now ends with "…" instead of being cut short.
