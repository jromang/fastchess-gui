Everything since 0.3.2: Chess960, opening books, test suites, ratings across tournaments, the SPRT calculator,
lichess broadcasts, engine checks and fair play, charts move by move, and pages that use the whole window.

## Changes

**Look and layout** (new in 0.4.2)
- **Pages that use the whole window**, large or small. The Live tab's mosaic picks its columns for the largest
  boards in the room it has; the followed board takes the full height, with the players, moves and charts beside
  it down to the bottom (under it in a narrow window). The new tournament form splits into two columns on a wide
  window. The list of the Engines, Books and Ratings pages takes a share of the width, widened by dragging its
  edge. The tournament overview has an Elo chart under the standings and the latest results down to the bottom.
- In a small window, one scroll bar at a time: the form, the replay and the engine options no longer put a second
  one beside the first; the rail's buttons get lower; rows of buttons and filters wrap instead of being cut.
- **Interface size**, from 80 to 250 %: *Settings*, or Ctrl + mouse wheel, Ctrl + and Ctrl -, Ctrl 0.
- A **logo**, a pawn whose head is a stopwatch: in the rail, as the window's icon and as the icon of the Windows
  executable.

**Tournaments and matches**
- **Chess960** (Fischer random), with UCI and WinBoard engines alike. Tick *Chess960* in the openings of a new
  tournament: without a book, the games start from the 960 starting positions in random order; with a book, from
  its positions. The engines must play Chess960 (`UCI_Chess960`, or WinBoard's `fischerandom` variant, positions in
  X-FEN): the summary names those that do not, and such a tournament does not start.
- **Time controls**: a fixed time per move, and a fixed depth (per participant too).
- **The same opening for every pair of a round**, in round robins, gauntlets and Swiss tournaments. The Schedule
  tab shows each encounter's opening.
- **SPRT calculator**, as fishtest's: the probability that H1 is accepted and the expected number of games against
  the true Elo difference, from a draw ratio and the RMS bias of the openings. The setup tells how many games its
  bounds are likely to take.
- **Match statistics**: a folded *Statistics* section with fishtest's numbers, from the games and from the pairs:
  Elo, nElo, BayesElo and DrawElo, standard deviation, LLR of both, variance ratio, RMS bias of the openings, kinds
  of balanced pairs. *Copy* includes them.
- **Fair play**: the processor time of every engine is measured while it thinks and while its opponent does; the
  Overview warns when an engine uses more cores than its threads, or thinks on its opponent's time.

**Engines**
- A **quick test** on each engine: 10 games at 10+0.1 from 5 positions, against itself or another engine, played
  like a real match. Its result stays with the engine: score, losses on time, crashes, illegal moves, and the share
  of its clock it used.
- *Check* runs fastchess' **UCI compliance** steps too, with a report kept with the engine.
- A **missing or rebuilt program** is noticed: a missing one is marked with *Locate…* and a tournament does not
  start with it; a rebuilt one has its options read again. Tournaments record the programs they were started with
  and warn when one changed since.
- **Engine logs**: every line exchanged with both engines is kept for the games lost on time, by a crash, a stall
  or an illegal move (or every game, if asked), in `logs/` of the tournament folder, opened from the Games tab.
- **Wrong PVs** (illegal PV moves, a PV going on after the end of the game, a best move that is not the PV's
  first, and with *Check the mate PVs* a mate score whose PV does not mate) are marked on their moves in the Games
  tab, which can show only the games that have one.
- **Win, draw and loss shares** of the engines that report them (`UCI_ShowWDL`, switched on for new engines that
  have it): after each search line, and as bands under the evaluation curve.
- **WinBoard engines**: an option *Scores from White's side* for old engines that report scores from White's point
  of view; an engine that asks to be started again for every game (`reuse=0`) gets *Restart the engine for every
  game* ticked when it is added; test suites run them too.

**Opening books**
- A **Books** page: a library of books (EPD, PGN, Polyglot), each checked for illegal positions, duplicates,
  positions with colours reversed and games already over, browsed on a board, and saved as a cleaned copy. The
  setup offers them by name.
- **Polyglot** `.bin` books, followed to a depth and played as positions.
- An **Openings** tab in each tournament: results by starting position, with the openings that favour a colour,
  those always drawn and those that tell the engines apart; exported as EPD.

**Test suites**
- A *Suites* page runs an EPD suite (STS, WAC, ERET…) with any number of engines: positions with their best move
  (`bm`), the moves to avoid (`am`) or STS points, at a time, nodes or depth per position. A grid of positions
  against engines, with the time to solution and the totals first (solved, average time, STS score); searches stop
  once a solution has held for a few plies. Finished runs are kept to compare later versions with, and the grid
  exports as CSV. An EPD book opens there from the Books page.

**Games**
- **Charts move by move**, chosen above the curve of the live view and of the replay, for both engines: time per
  move with the **decision time** (when the engine settled on its move), depth and seldepth, nodes, speed,
  tablebase hits, and how far the two engines' PVs agree.
- Under every board, the **50-move counter**, the material difference and the pieces left, marked *TB* when the
  tablebase adjudication can end the game (in the mosaic, when asked).
- The **opening** of every game (ECO code and name) in the Games tab, and a filter by code, range or name.
- **Interesting games**: filters for the games where the engines disagree, where an evaluation swings, or that
  were lost from a winning score, each opened at that moment.
- The engines' reference **Elo in the PGN tags** (`WhiteElo`, `BlackElo`).

**Ratings**
- A **Ratings** page over every game of every tournament, in four tabs: the **rating list** (minimum number of
  games, an export for Ordo and BayesElo), an **engine's card** (results by time control, losses on time, crashes
  and illegal moves, its tournaments, search statistics, its rating over time), **head to head** records, and the
  **progress** of an engine's versions.
- Filters by time control, group of the engine library and period, and games of **imported PGN files** (CCRL,
  CEGT, other programs).

**Broadcast and sharing**
- **lichess broadcasts**: the games pushed to a lichess broadcast with a personal API token (in *Settings*, or the
  Broadcast tab), on lichess' board, evaluation bar and chat; a broadcast named after the tournament is made, or
  the games go to a given round. Headless mode: `--lichess new` or `--lichess ROUND`, with the token in
  `FCG_LICHESS_TOKEN`. HTTPS through Mbed TLS, built in, trusting only the authorities that sign lichess.org.
- **TLCS**, tried with node-tlcv (the viewer behind ccrl.live): the moves come one at a time and in order; the
  clocks, PVs and crosstable are sent as TLCS sends them; the crosstable lists the games played, from which
  node-tlcv numbers its games; the players are named before the game starts, so its PGN gets their names. The
  engines no longer inherit the server's sockets.
- *Copy* as text or as **BBCode** for a forum post: standings, crosstable, match result, rating list.

**Fixes**
- A tab asked for while a tournament starts (from an engine's card, for instance) is no longer sometimes lost.

## Known limitations

- A lichess round holds 64 games; the next ones go to a new round of the same broadcast. lichess keeps only the
  moves and clocks of a broadcast: the engines' evaluations do not show there, its evaluation bar is its own.
- The lichess connection does not go through a proxy, and trusts only Google Trust Services and Let's Encrypt:
  should lichess change authority, a new version is needed.
- Charts of the replay need their fields in the PGN (nodes, seldepth, nps, pv, tbhits, decision time are PGN
  options of the setup); the live view always has them.
- The engines of imported PGN files are known by the names the files give them; they are not matched to the
  engine library.
- Engine logs hold what the engines write on their standard output, not their error output.
- Processor use is measured while games are played and starts again when a tournament is resumed.
- WinBoard engines of protocol 1 without `setboard` nor edit mode can only start from the initial position.
- The TLCS server has been tried with node-tlcv, not with Tom's Live Chess Viewer itself, which does not connect
  under Wine (its socket cannot take the broadcast port).
- Ctrl + mouse wheel also zooms a chart under the mouse.
- On Wayland, detached boards stay inside the main window.
